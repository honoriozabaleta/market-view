# Institutional research publication map

**Study edition — checked on 20 October 2026.**

This is the owner's single reference document for studying the research available from the five institutions already considered for Market View: J.P. Morgan, UBS, BlackRock, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. The scope now spans their research, wealth and asset management businesses, rather than only the five streams selected for the earlier extraction pilot. BlackRock is an asset manager; the other four are banking groups with several research-producing businesses.

The purpose is to understand the material before choosing a product. The entries describe publication families, report formats and access channels. They are not a complete catalogue of every report, a recovered historical archive, an endorsement of a current investment view or a decision to ingest a source.

## How to read the map

The useful identity is **institution → business/division → team → publication family → dated edition → attributed statement**. An economist's forecast, a private bank's allocation and a portfolio manager's commentary can differ without either being an extraction error.

Three classification axes overlap:

| Axis     | Examples                                                                         | What it tells you                       |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Producer | Sell-side Research; Wealth Management CIO; Asset Management investment team      | Who is speaking and for which audience. |
| Subject  | Macroeconomics; allocation; rates; credit; equities; currencies; private markets | What the analysis covers.               |
| Package  | Outlook; weekly note; chartbook; thematic paper; podcast; dashboard              | How the analysis is delivered.          |

A macro outlook can therefore be an annual edition of a named economics series. A podcast may discuss the same report. These are not independent institutional opinions or additional votes.

Access labels describe what was established during this survey:

| Label                 | Meaning                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Public page**       | An official entry page or sample was retrieved without signing in. This does not establish unrestricted access to every attachment, region or edition.   |
| **Listing / summary** | An official directory, introduction or link identifies the material. The complete report or archive was not verified.                                    |
| **Client channel**    | An official page identifies client access or a research portal. The public description is useful for discovery; the underlying service was not accessed. |
| **Historical sample** | A dated older example establishes the type of material. A current equivalent or continuous series remains unverified.                                    |

Cadence is stated conservatively: “quarter-labelled” or “monthly-labelled” describes observed packaging; it does not promise that every edition exists or is publicly available. **Publication cadence is not the forecast or allocation horizon.** Where no explicit horizon is established below, inspect the individual edition.

## Document types and what they contain

This taxonomy is an analytical guide written for this project. The institutions do not necessarily use the same labels.

| Type                                    | Main content to look for                                                                 | Research fields it may support                                                               | Main reading trap                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Macro outlook                           | Growth, inflation, employment, monetary/fiscal policy, central and alternative scenarios | Indicator, geography, forecast value/range, unit, target period, measurement basis, scenario | A macro scenario does not itself disclose an asset allocation.                                         |
| Cross-asset / house view                | Relative preferences across asset classes and regions, portfolio risks                   | Original allocation label, benchmark/reference, universe, horizon, thesis, risk              | “House view” belongs to the named team and context, not automatically the whole group.                 |
| Asset-class strategy                    | Rates/duration, credit, FX, equities, commodities or alternatives                        | Instrument/universe, relative trade, target, valuation argument, catalyst                    | A currency basket or curve trade is not a broad asset-class stance.                                    |
| Annual / midyear outlook                | A larger synthesis of macro, themes and investment implications                          | Separate macro, asset and portfolio statements within one edition                            | The cover's target year can differ from the publication year.                                          |
| Weekly / daily update                   | New data, policy events, earnings, changes in a tactical assessment                      | Dated statement, actual revision, event response, source links                               | Omission is not reaffirmation, withdrawal or neutral.                                                  |
| Chartbook / data guide                  | Economic and market charts, valuations, returns and historical context                   | Chart definition, data source, as-of date, universe, units                                   | A historical chart is not a forecast; chart dates can precede the report date.                         |
| Capital market assumptions (CMAs)       | Expected long-run returns, volatility, correlations and strategic portfolio inputs       | Currency, hedging, nominal/real basis, horizon, methodology, asset universe                  | A long-run expected return is not a near-term price target or tactical weight.                         |
| Thematic / structural paper             | Technology, demographics, geopolitics, sustainability, industry transformation           | Theme, causal argument, affected sectors, risks, time frame                                  | A structural opportunity is not necessarily a current overweight.                                      |
| Company / sector research               | Earnings estimates, valuation, ratings, industry developments                            | Issuer, estimate period, rating scale, target price, analyst/team                            | A stock rating or tactical idea does not represent broad equity allocation.                            |
| Portfolio-construction paper            | Diversification, risk budgets, factors, implementation, taxes, liquidity                 | Assumptions, constraints, illustrative allocation, model purpose                             | An illustration or model portfolio is not evidence of actual holdings.                                 |
| Investor / family-office survey         | Respondents' allocations, intentions, constraints and concerns                           | Sample, fieldwork dates, weighting, response definitions                                     | Respondents' positions are not the publisher's recommendations.                                        |
| Historical returns / wealth study       | Long-run market returns, wealth levels and distribution                                  | Dataset, geography, sampling, historical period, methodology                                 | Historical evidence is different from the institution's forward-looking view.                          |
| Dashboard / dataset / valuation tool    | Indicators, models, alternative data, downloadable assumptions                           | Methodology/version, observation time, units, coverage                                       | A tool is not a stable dated report or an additional research stream.                                  |
| Audio / video / newsletter              | Discussions, summaries, interviews and links to underlying research                      | Speaker, affiliation, episode date, transcript timestamp, linked report                      | Guests and quoted analysts must retain their own attribution.                                          |
| Fund / corporate / regulatory documents | Mandates, holdings, results, fees, risks, stewardship or corporate accounts              | Product-specific or issuer-specific facts, if relevant to a future product                   | Factsheets, prospectuses and corporate annual reports answer different questions from market outlooks. |

## Suggested study sequence

This sequence is intended to help the owner inspect the source material before deciding on product design. It does not select new default streams or approve extraction.

1. Read one chartbook and one macro outlook. Identify which elements are observed data, forecasts and interpretation.
2. Read one cross-asset allocation publication from each institution where accessible. Write down the original scale, portfolio reference and horizon.
3. Within one institution, compare Research, Wealth and AM material. Note differences in question, audience, assumptions and authorship.
4. Read two CMA documents with different horizons. Inspect methodology rather than ranking their return numbers directly.
5. Follow a weekly update back to its underlying outlook. Check whether it changes, reiterates or merely discusses an earlier position.
6. Read one thematic paper and one investor survey. Separate the authors' analysis from respondents' or guests' views.
7. Only then decide which information is sufficiently useful, accessible and repeatable for the next product.

Use this worksheet inside this document or in personal notes:

| Reading question                  | What to record                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who speaks?                       | Institution, division, team, author; external contributors.                          |
| What question does it answer?     | Macro, allocation, target, valuation, structural theme, implementation or survey.    |
| How explicit is it?               | Exact source label/table versus narrative; missing reference/horizon.                |
| What is its clock?                | Publication date, edition, data as-of dates, forecast target period, capture time.   |
| Can I access it repeatedly?       | Public page/full report, client restriction, archive, mutable URL, regional variant. |
| What can I extract defensibly?    | Candidate fields and evidence locators; keep interpretation separate.                |
| What does it leave unanswered?    | Missing assets/topics, unread exhibits, unknown units/bases, access limits.          |
| Would a product need this family? | Owner's conclusion after reading; no selection is pre-filled here.                   |
